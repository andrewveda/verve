Tenses

How verbs tell us when — and how — things happen.

A tense is the form a verb takes to show when an action happens — in the past, the present, or the future. Change the tense and the same action moves through time: I walk, I walked, I will walk.

Time and aspect

English tenses combine two ideas: time (past, present, future) and aspect (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). Three times times four aspects gives twelve tenses in all.

In focus

Four forms of the past tense

I

Past simple

verb + -ed  /  irregular form

A finished action at a definite time in the past. The most common past tense.

She visited Paris last year.
The visit is over; the time is known.
II

Past continuous

was / were + verb-ing

An action in progress at a moment in the past, or one interrupted by another.

I was cooking when the phone rang.
Cooking was ongoing; the call interrupted it.
III

Past perfect

had + past participle

An action completed before another past action — the "earlier past".

The film had started before we arrived.
Started first; arrived second.
IV

Past perfect continuous

had been + verb-ing

An action that had been going on for some time before a past moment.

He had been waiting for an hour when she came.
The waiting lasted until she arrived.
Know the difference

Regular vs. irregular verbs

Regular verbs

Add -ed to form both the past simple and the past participle.

walk → walked → walked

Irregular verbs

Change form unpredictably, so they must be learned one by one.

go → went → gone
  • be → was/were → been
  • have → had → had
  • see → saw → seen
  • take → took → taken
  • write → wrote → written
  • put → put → put
Past vs. present

Telling them apart

Past simple

The action is finished, usually with a stated time.

"I lived in Chennai in 2015."

Present perfect

The action connects to now, with no specific time given.

"I have lived in Chennai for years."
