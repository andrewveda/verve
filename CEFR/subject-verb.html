Subject-Verb Agreement

One rule, a handful of traps.

Ready to check your agreement?

Take the test Learn more

Subject-verb agreement means the verb must match its subject in number. A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Singular

The dog barks.

Plural

The dogs bark.
The core trick

In the present tense, a singular third-person subject (he, she, it) adds -s to the verb. The noun's plural takes -s; the verb's does not.

Five traps

Where agreement goes wrong

I

Words in between

Ignore extra phrases. Find the true subject.

The box of chocolates is empty.
Subject: box, not chocolates.
II

Joined with "and"

Two subjects joined by and are usually plural.

Tea and coffee are ready.
Exception: one idea, as in "Rice and curry is my favourite."
III

Joined with "or" / "nor"

The verb agrees with the subject closest to it.

Neither the teacher nor the students were late.
Nearest subject: students.
IV

Indefinite pronouns

Everyone, someone, each, nobody look plural but take singular verbs.

Everyone has a ticket.
Not "have".
V

Collective nouns

Team, family, committee take a singular verb when acting as one unit.

The team wins every year.
One group, one verb.
