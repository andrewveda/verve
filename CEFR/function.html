The architecture of sentences

Words that carry the weight, and words that do the work.

Every sentence you speak is a collaboration between meaning and structure.

English vocabulary is divided into two distinct tribes. Content words are the bricks—they give us the picture. Function words are the mortar—they give us the grammatical frame that holds the bricks together.

Why it matters

If you lose the function words, you sound like a telegram (Dog bit man). If you lose the content words, you are left with grammatical ghosts (The... did it to the...).

The Breakdown

The Two Tribes

Content Words

They carry the actual information, imagery, and emotion of a sentence. Because language evolves, this is an open class—we invent new content words every day (like selfie or doomscroll).

Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives, Adverbs

Function Words

They connect, point, and relate. They are the structural glue. This is a closed class—the list is fixed, and we almost never invent new prepositions or articles.

The Telegram Test

Meaning survives the loss of grammar, but grammar means nothing without content.

Notice what happens when we strip a sentence down to just one tribe or the other:

  • The old man walked to the store.Old man walked store. (Content words only: Clunky, but the message survives.)
  • She is waiting at the station.She is... at the... (Function words only: Flawless grammar, zero meaning.)
