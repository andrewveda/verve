WH Questions

The questions that open the door — and how they differ from Yes/No.

A WH question is a type of question that asks for specific information — a person, a thing, a time, a place, a reason, or a manner. It cannot be answered with just "yes" or "no"; the speaker is asking you to supply something new.

Also called

An open question or information question. It takes its name from the question words, most of which begin with wh — who, what, when, where, why, which — plus how, which joins the family despite spelling.

Most WH questions put the question word first, followed by a helping verb and then the subject: Where do you live? The opening word tells the listener exactly what kind of answer is wanted.

A taxonomy

Nine question words, nine kinds of answer

I

Who

Asks about a person — the subject of an action.

Who called you this morning?
My sister did.
II

Whom

Asks about the person receiving an action. Formal; in everyday speech, who often replaces it.

Whom did you invite?
The whole team.
III

Whose

Asks about ownership or belonging.

Whose umbrella is this?
It's Maya's.
IV

What

Asks about a thing, an idea, or an action. The most open-ended of them all.

What are you reading?
A novel about the sea.
V

Which

Asks you to choose from a known set of options.

Which route do you prefer?
The coastal one.
VI

When

Asks about time — a moment, a date, or a period.

When does the class begin?
At half past nine.
VII

Where

Asks about place or direction.

Where did you park?
Behind the library.
VIII

Why

Asks for a reason or a cause. These answers often begin with because.

Why did you leave early?
Because the train was delayed.
IX

How

Asks about manner, method, degree, or condition — and pairs with other words: how much, how many, how long, how often.

How did you fix it?
I restarted the whole system.
Know the difference

WH questions vs. Yes/No questions

Yes/No question

A closed question. It allows only two replies and starts with a helping verb.

"Did you enjoy the film?"

WH question

An open question. It invites a full answer and starts with a question word.

"What did you think of the film?"

Yes/No: Do you like coffee?
WH: What do you like to drink in the morning?
One checks a fact; the other opens the topic.
Yes/No: Is the meeting at noon?
WH: When is the meeting?
The WH version asks for the time instead of testing a guess.
Yes/No: Did the plan fail?
WH: Why did the plan fail?
"Yes" settles the fact. "Why" reaches the cause.
Yes/No: Can you help me?
WH: How can you help me?
The WH form invites ideas, not just agreement.
