A multi-stage innovation challenge. Think deeply, investigate intelligently, design purposefully, build boldly and defend your solution.

Six stages · One team · One meaningful solution

The challenge

A hackathon is not a race to write the most code. It is a test of how well you can understand a real problem, question assumptions, develop a feasible response and demonstrate that it works.

The journey

Six stages. One innovation journey.

Open each stage to explore the tasks, expectations and submission requirements. Complete each stage before moving to the next.

Entry stage

Build your team and formally enter the challenge.

Your team must

  • Register a distinctive team name.
  • Form a team of six members.
  • Appoint a team leader and identify a faculty mentor.
  • Assign preliminary roles: research, design, development, documentation, presentation and testing.
  • Select one problem statement from the challenge list.
Submission: Team registration form, member details, role allocation and selected problem statement.
First elimination round

This is the first major evaluation. Your team must prove that it understands the problem, the context in which it exists and the people affected by it. Do not jump straight to a solution.

Task A · Brainstorm

  • What exactly is the problem?
  • Who experiences it, and in what circumstances?
  • Why does the problem exist?
  • What solutions already exist? Why are they insufficient?
  • What happens if the problem remains unsolved?

Task B · Define the problem

  • Rewrite the problem statement in your own words.
  • Identify target users and stakeholders.
  • Investigate root causes; distinguish symptoms from causes.
  • Support your understanding with research, examples or evidence.

Task C · Establish the boundaries

  • Expected outcomes: What should a successful solution achieve?
  • Functional requirements: What must it do?
  • Constraints: Budget, time, infrastructure, technology, accessibility and connectivity.
  • Assumptions: What are you taking for granted?
  • Success criteria: How will you measure whether it works?

Task D · Create your submission

Create a professional webpage or presentation titled Problem Discovery and Solution Blueprint. It must include:

  1. Problem statement and background
  2. Target audience and stakeholders
  3. Root-cause analysis
  4. Existing solutions and their limitations
  5. Expected outcomes
  6. Functional and non-functional requirements
  7. Constraints and assumptions
  8. Preliminary solution direction
  9. Measurable success criteria
  10. Research sources and evidence
Submission: A webpage or PPT that demonstrates a thorough, evidence-informed understanding of the problem.
Shortlisting principle: Repeating the problem statement is not analysis. Show independent thinking, meaningful investigation and clarity.
Second elimination round

Shortlisted teams now move from understanding the problem to designing a credible solution.

Your team must

  • Generate at least three possible solutions.
  • Compare alternatives and justify the chosen approach.
  • Draw a system architecture, process flow or user journey.
  • Identify the technologies, tools and platforms required.
  • Break the solution into modules and assign responsibilities.
  • Explain how users will interact with the solution.
  • Identify technical challenges, dependencies and risks.
Submission: Solution blueprint, architecture/workflow diagram, technology stack and implementation plan.
Judgement: Is the solution innovative, practical, scalable and technically feasible?
Build phase

The blueprint is approved. Now turn the idea into something people can see, use and test.

Your team must

  • Set up the development environment and shared repository.
  • Divide work into manageable tasks and milestones.
  • Build the core features first.
  • Develop the user interface and required backend/database components.
  • Integrate the modules and maintain regular progress updates.
  • Document important technical decisions and challenges.
Submission: A functional prototype or Minimum Viable Product (MVP), with a repository and brief progress log.

A prototype is not proof of success. Test whether it solves the problem it was designed to solve.

Your team must

  • Test all major features and identify bugs.
  • Run realistic user scenarios and, where possible, gather user feedback.
  • Check the solution against the original expected outcomes.
  • Measure performance against the success criteria.
  • Improve the prototype based on findings.
  • Prepare a reliable demonstration and document limitations.
Submission: Tested prototype, test report, feedback and evidence of improvements.
Judgement: Does it actually solve the problem, or does it merely look impressive?
Final evaluation

Present your work to the jury. Make a compelling case for why your solution deserves to exist beyond this hackathon.

Your final presentation must include

  • The problem and why it matters.
  • Target users and their needs.
  • The proposed solution and its innovation.
  • System architecture and technology stack.
  • A live demonstration of the prototype.
  • Testing results and measurable outcomes.
  • Limitations, future improvements and scalability.
  • Potential for real-world implementation.

Prepare for the jury

  • Why did you choose this approach?
  • What makes your solution different?
  • What if your assumptions are wrong?
  • Can it work at scale?
  • What would you improve with another month?
Submission: Final pitch, live prototype demonstration and technical defence.
The objective: Convince the jury that your solution is useful, feasible and ready for the next step.

Make it an experience

Bring the hackathon atmosphere to campus.

The people

  • SPOC: Coordinates registration, submissions and communication.
  • Jury: Faculty across departments and, where possible, industry experts.
  • Mentors: Guide teams without building the solution for them.

The experience

  • Submission portal: Host team profiles, webpages, presentations and progress reports.
  • Innovation wall: Showcase each team's problem and proposed solution.
  • Jury interrogation: Invite questions at every elimination stage.
